Case study 01 · Naming and brand design

House of Assembly

Naming and brand identity for a house music rave that celebrates Nigerian house music and the community growing around it. Three editions so far, with roughly 5,000+ people through the doors.

My roleNaming, brand design
OrganisersWeTalkSound · Cafe Riddim
Editions3 terms, Lagos
Attendance~5,000+ combined (approx.)
The brief

A house music rave dedicated to celebrating Nigerian house music and the community growing around it. The event is meant to be a cultural meeting point for DJs, producers, dancers, creatives and music lovers, so the name and identity had to feel like a place people belong to.

The name

"House of Assembly" works on two levels. In Nigeria it is the name of a state's legislature, a place where people gather to represent their communities. It also says exactly what the event is: people assembling through house music.

The political idea became a naming system. Each edition is a "Term" (First Term, 2nd Term, 3rd Term), and the 3rd Term line-up was billed as "Honourables".

The identity
  1. Wordmark
    Drawn by hand first, then refined into a heavy arched wordmark with the weight of an official seal.
  2. Crest
    A coat of arms in the style of an official seal: a starred shield, two leopards and a crown. It was rendered in 3D for screens and the venue.
  3. Illustration
    A hand-drawn assembly chamber where the seats are filled with DJs, decks and dancers.
  4. Colour
    A deep green, two electric greens and white, used at high contrast so the brand holds up in a dark venue and on a phone screen.
    #027122#B6E100#FEFF01#FFFFFF
The system at work

The identity had to run across every touchpoint of a live event, and come back fresh for each new term without losing what people recognised.

On the night

The crest was built into the venue as a light-up centrepiece, and the brand mark sat on every photo that went out after each event.

Outcome

House of Assembly has run three editions in Lagos, at The Vault and Mantra Lounge in Victoria Island, with roughly 5,000+ attendees combined (approximate). Each term reused the same name, crest and colours while the posters, line-up and naming moved forward.

What this shows
  • Finding one strong idea and turning it into a full naming and visual system.
  • Designing for a community: the brand gives people something to belong to.
  • A system that scaled across three editions and dozens of touchpoints, from wristbands to video.

Case study 02 · Print, visual and motion design

The Republic V10: N2 – Nigeria in the World

Print, visual and motion design for an issue of The Republic, carried from the printed magazine to social media and a launch film.

My rolePrint, visual, motion design
PublicationThe Republic
IssueVol. 10 No. 2 · May–Jul 2026
TeamDesign with Michael Emono · Illustration by Shalom Ojo
The brief

"Nigeria in the World" explores how Nigeria moves through the world, and how the world in turn shapes and misreads Nigeria. Across Africa, Asia, Europe and the Americas, the issue traces the political, cultural and economic encounters behind that relationship and their consequences at home.

The design had to hold a big, global subject together in one issue, then carry it beyond print into the places readers actually find The Republic.

Approach

Build one illustrated world and use it everywhere. The cover gathers Nigerian figures inside arches and foliage, and the back cover continues the same scene. A muted palette of sage, brown and cream ties the print, social and motion pieces together.

The work
  1. Cover
    A wraparound cover, front to back across the spine, with masthead, issue details and price set into the illustration.
  2. Features
    Spreads for the issue's stories. For "The Lesser-Told Story of Nigerians in Ireland", I also made the photo illustration.
  3. Social
    One card template for each article: masthead, headline, standfirst and byline, with each story's own image and colour.
  4. Motion
    A launch film and portrait crops cut from the cover, so the issue could be announced across channels.
Outcome

The issue went to print and was published for May to July 2026, with social cards and a launch film supporting it online.

What this shows
  • Turning a complex, many-sided subject into one clear visual story.
  • Designing templates and rules that scale across many articles, like components in a product.
  • Taking one idea across print, social and motion, and working closely with an illustrator and a co-designer.

Case study 03 · Art direction

ATSG Vol. 1: Reminisce

Art direction for Alaye Toh Se Gogo, Vol. 1, Reminisce's first LP in seven years. One visual system carries the album cover, the back cover and eleven single covers.

My roleArt direction
StudioWeTalkSound
Artist / labelReminisce · LRR Music
PhotographyBadmantej
The brief

ATSG, Vol. 1 is Reminisce's first LP in seven years. The album pairs street insight with a wide range of collaborators, from Olamide and Mayorkun to Oxlade, Bnxn and Odumodublvck. Its throughline is consistent growth: Reminisce as a boundary-breaker and a bridge between generations of Nigerian rap.

The artwork had to announce a comeback, and sit naturally beside the newer artists on the record.

Concept

Treat the album like a movie poster for an icon. A tight, dramatic portrait shows his poise and presence, and that single image sets the tone for the whole project.

The system
  1. Portrait
    A low-key black-and-white portrait in profile. The cover and back cover face opposite directions, so the pair reads as one spread.
  2. Red window
    One vertical red panel cuts through every artwork. On the cover it frames his face. On the singles it becomes a window onto that song's image.
  3. Type
    Heavy condensed capitals for titles, a small spaced-out artist name, and the album name set large and faded in the background.
  4. Texture
    A cracked, worn black surface behind everything, which keeps eleven different images feeling like one album.
Singles

Each single keeps the same frame and swaps in one symbol for the track: eyes for "Eyes", a chair for "Hustle", a cassette for "Shina Peters", chains for "Tight". The system makes every release recognisable at a glance in a streaming feed, while each cover still has its own idea.

Outcome
To fill, facts onlyRelease year, and anything you can point to: where the artwork ran (streaming, billboards, merch, social), press coverage, or response from the artist and label.
What this shows
  • Turning an artist's story into one clear creative concept.
  • Designing a system with fixed rules and flexible content, the same thinking behind a component library.
  • Keeping 13 deliverables consistent across formats and collaborators.
